Dawnbreak City stands on the east-facing Atlantic coast of Virginia, at the
mouth of the Nashoke River, and holds a little over a quarter of a million
people. It was founded in 1921 by people who needed somewhere to go. It is also
the city where BEV-131 was released twelve years ago, and most of what visitors
know about it begins there.

## The Place

The harbor is the reason for everything else. The Nashoke comes down out of the
western marshes, widens, turns south, and empties into water deep enough to take
ocean freight, and the founders laid their city out around that turn. The core
districts still follow the plan they drew. [[The Old Financial District]] holds
the east bank, [[Aldergate]] the west, [[Tidewater Commons]] the ground south of
Aldergate, and [[Grid Row]] runs northeast along the rail line. [[New Haven]] is
the exception and the newest thing on the map.

Everything beyond that plan was absorbed rather than designed. [[Cape Crooked]]
was a beach resort with its own post office before the city annexed it in 1931.
[[Folded Ridge Point]] came in during the war, in 1943, as a federal airfield on
land Dawnbreak had never wanted. [[Fenmoor Flats]] was farm country and a
scatter of houses until 1948. The three of them do not match the street grid
around them, do not share its assumptions about where a road ought to go, and
have never entirely joined the city that surrounds them.

Ten districts, then, and [[The Undercity]] beneath all of them, which appears on
no public map and is described in municipal documents as utilities.

### Ground and water

The land here is sinking. It has been sinking since before the city was built,
slowly and unevenly, and the sea has spent the same century coming up to meet
it. Dawnbreak therefore floods on days with no weather in them. A spring tide
will put water across the low end of Bay Street in sunshine, and has done for
years.

This has sorted the city. [[Elysian Heights]] sits forty feet above the harbor
and does not think about tides. Aldergate is high enough. The Old Financial
District, built on the oldest and lowest ground in the city, floods at its edges
most autumns. Cape Crooked is a sandbar with houses on it. Tidewater Commons
takes water across its playing fields several times a year. Fenmoor Flats is
named for what it is.

The city's answer is the Harborworks programme, begun in 2031 and still
building. Its centrepiece is a floodwall along the harbor frontage, tied into
raised roadway and pumping stations, and by the standards of the engineering it
works. What the wall protects is the harbor frontage, the Old Financial
District, New Haven, and the generating station and freight terminal in
[[Grid Row]]. It turns inland at the Tidewater Commons boundary. Fenmoor Flats
and Cape Crooked lie outside it, scheduled for a later phase that has been
rescheduled twice.

The route through Grid Row follows the substations rather than the streets.
Residents there have noticed, and say so at every hearing. The city's position
is that the wall was drawn to keep the lights on for everybody, which is
accurate and is not quite an answer to the question being asked.

New Haven was raised on engineered ground before a single tower went up on it,
which is why it stayed dry through the 2036 surge and the district it replaced
did not.

### How the city is run

Dawnbreak is an independent city. It sits inside no county, answers to no county
government, and administers its own courts, schools, police, water and roads
through a council of nine and a manager the council appoints. The mayor sits on
the council and chairs it.

No district has a government of its own, which surprises people who move here.
What a district has instead is a civic league: a voluntary association of
residents that holds no authority, levies nothing, and cannot compel the city to
do anything whatsoever. The city keeps a list of the leagues and uses it to send
notices and to ask opinions before it acts. Leagues that are well run and well
attended get consulted early and often. The rest get consulted after the
decision. Nothing in the charter requires either.

### Getting around

Two bridges cross the Nashoke inside the city, and the Harbor Tunnel carries the
southern highway under the channel to Folded Ridge Point. Buses run everywhere
and run slowly. The rail line through Grid Row has been freight and nothing else
since 1926.

## H-Day and After

On **11 June 2028**, BEV-131 was released over the Old Financial District. What
happened that day belongs to [[H-Day]]. What belongs here is what it did to the
shape of the city.

The financial district emptied and never refilled. Banking, insurance and
corporate headquarters relocated during the reconstruction and consolidated into
New Haven, which was built to receive them on raised ground north of the harbor
and has held that role since. The old district was rebuilt to its original
footprint, occupied by different people, and left carrying a name for a job it
no longer does.

Harborworks was authorised three years after that. City engineers had been
asking for a floodwall since the 1990s and had been refused every time on cost.
The plan that finally passed was drawn around a district that had just been
rebuilt from nothing and a district that had just been built from nothing, and
the flood case is difficult to separate from the fact that the city had recently
spent a great deal of money on both.

## Who's There

Around 258,000 people, a figure that has moved very little in twenty years and
conceals a good deal of movement inside it. The city loses households to the
suburbs and gains them from much further away, which is roughly what it was
built to do.

Metahumans live here in numbers nobody has counted accurately. Registration with
[[The Cleaners]] exists and is poorly subscribed. What Dawnbreak has instead of a
count is [[Pantheon]], six people employed by [[Arkon Media]] and visible enough
to stand in for everybody else, and a much larger population of the changed who
went back to their jobs and said nothing about it.

Policing is the [[Dawnbreak City Police]], with the Metahuman Containment Unit
for calls that need it. The federal presence is the
[[Department of Metahuman Damage Mitigation]], whose aircraft use the federal
field at Folded Ridge Point alongside the Coast Guard station there. Dawnbreak is
not a Navy town and never has been.

## The Public Record

The founding is well documented. The Dawnbreak Company was formed in New York in
1920 by people who had concluded that leaving was safer than staying. It bought
tidewater land cheaply, because it was tidewater land, and platted a city on it
the following year. The name was chosen for the sun coming up over the water,
and the company's prospectus says so in as many words.

Tide data is public and has been collected here continuously since 1954. The
subsidence figures are public. The Harborworks phasing schedule is public, as are
the minutes of every meeting at which a phase was deferred.

The city's account of itself has not changed in a century: Dawnbreak was built to
be a place people could arrive at, and it still is. Nothing in the public record
contradicts that. What the record holds instead is a long series of ordinary
decisions about where to spend money, each of them defensible on its own.

## Contested / unconfirmed

**Whether the Harborworks phasing follows the engineering.** The city holds that
the wall went where the water arrives first and the assets sit thickest, which is
true. Residents of the deferred districts observe that the water arrives at them
too, that it always has, and that their phase keeps moving. Both positions cite
the same survey.

**Whether the population figure is real.** The count has been stable for two
decades, which several people who work with the numbers consider too stable for a
city with this much churn. There is no agreement about who is going uncounted, or
why.
