Fenmoor Flats is the western end of [[Dawnbreak City]], between the river's
upper reach and the marsh country the city has never built on. It was farmland
and a scattering of houses until the city annexed it in 1948. It is outside the
Harborworks floodwall, it is scheduled for a phase that has moved twice, and it
is further from any hero's usual ground than anywhere else on the map.

## The Place

Low, flat and wide. The streets here run to the horizon and stop at the
greenbelt, and the housing is mostly single-storey or low terrace with yards
around it, built quickly in the twenty years after annexation for people who
wanted room and could not afford Aldergate.

The airfield sits on the district's northern flank, a general aviation field
with two runways and a flight school, busy in daylight and silent otherwise. It
is the last thing on the map before the marsh.

Nothing about the Flats reads as city. The lots are too big, the roads are too
straight, and the sky is much larger here than anywhere east of the river.
Residents consider this the entire point. People who live in the core districts
tend to describe the Flats as somewhere you drive through, which is accurate,
because the western highway does exactly that.

### The ground

The name is not decorative. This is fen and moor: reclaimed marsh at the eastern
end, unreclaimed marsh at the western, and the boundary between them moves. The
whole district sits within a few feet of the tide line, and the water arrives
from underneath as often as it arrives from the river.

Fenmoor Flats floods in a way the rest of the city does not quite have a word
for. It is rarely dramatic. Yards go soft, the water table comes up through the
lowest floors, roads that were fine last year hold puddles that never dry, and
the effect on a house arrives over decades rather than in a night. There is no
surge here to photograph.

The Harborworks programme puts the Flats in a later phase, and that phase has
been rescheduled twice. The city's case is straightforward and is not made in bad
faith: the wall was built where the water arrives fastest and the value is
densest, this district has neither, and defending it means a different kind of
engineering entirely, closer to drainage and pumping than to a wall. The
district's case is equally straightforward. The water is arriving now.

### After the annexation

The city took the Flats in 1948 and spent the following decade putting in the
things a district needs. Water, sewers, two schools, a fire station and a bus
route arrived between 1949 and 1961, which the district remembers as the period
when Dawnbreak was interested in it.

Very little municipal building has happened here since. The schools are the
original ones, extended. The bus route is the original route, less frequent.

## H-Day and After

Nothing happened here on 11 June 2028. The Flats are well west and upwind of the
[[The Old Financial District|financial district]], and no effect of any kind was
recorded in the district that day or afterwards.

That has shaped the place more than it sounds. Dawnbreak reorganised itself
around H-Day, and a district with no part in it has spent twelve years listening
to a city talk about something that happened somewhere else. The Flats' relation
to H-Day is that it does not have one, and residents raise this at hearings when
the subject is money.

The one lasting change was arrivals. Households priced out of the rebuilt core
came west through the 2030s because the Flats were cheap and dry enough, which
is the only period of population growth the district has had since the sixties.

## Who's There

Around 21,000 people. Older than the city average, more likely to own their home
and less likely to be able to sell it, since flood disclosure on a Fenmoor
property is a serious matter and the buyers who remain are the buyers who intend
to stay a long time.

Work is mostly outside the district. The airfield, the schools, the two
industrial yards on the northern road and the shops on Fen Street employ some
people locally, and everybody else drives east.

The civic league exists and meets. Attendance rises sharply whenever a
Harborworks phase is discussed and falls again afterwards, which the league's
own minutes note with some frustration.

## The Public Record

The annexation record is complete and the 1949 to 1961 capital programme is
documented in detail, which makes the contrast with the following sixty years
easy for anybody to look up, and the league does.

Groundwater levels have been monitored at three points in the district since
1972. The readings are public. The Harborworks phasing schedule is public, as are
the two decisions to defer this district's phase and the reasons recorded for
each.
