New Haven occupies the north side of the harbor and did not exist before 2029.
It was built to hold the business that left the [[The Old Financial District|Old
Financial District]] after H-Day, on ground raised and drained for the purpose,
and it is the only district in [[Dawnbreak City]] whose entire history fits
inside living memory.

## The Place

Wide streets on a gentle curve, towers set back behind plazas, and a great deal
of glass. The district was designed as a single piece by people who knew what
was going into it, which no other part of Dawnbreak can say. Nothing here bends
around a rail line or a colonial property boundary. Where the streets curve,
they curve because a drawing said so.

The effect is deliberate and reads as intended. New Haven is aggressively
respectable. It is clean, it is well lit, it is patrolled, and the ground floors
along the main axis are given over to the kind of business that expects its
customers to have arrived by appointment.

### Made ground

The district sits on fill. Before anything was built the site was raised,
drained and stabilised, with the pumping capacity designed in rather than added
later, and the finished grade sits well above the old shoreline. This was the
most expensive part of the project and it was the part nobody argued about,
because the tidal record was already clear by 2029.

It worked. The 2036 surge put water through the low edge of the district New
Haven replaced and did not reach New Haven's street level anywhere. That
performance is the strongest argument in the city for building this way, and it
is also the reason residents of districts still waiting for a floodwall phase
tend to bring the surge up first.

### The towers

**Olympus Tower** is [[Arkon Media]]'s building and the tallest thing in the
city. Arkon's offices occupy the bulk of it. The top two floors are the
headquarters and living quarters of [[Pantheon]], which Arkon has never treated
as a secret and has in fact filmed from repeatedly. The Arkon Media campus
spreads around the tower's base, with a plaza large enough to take a crowd, a
press pen and a queue of applicants at the same time, which is a set of
requirements the architects were given in writing.

The **Cross Idea Network** tower stands close enough that its lobby looks
straight across at the Arkon plaza. It is smaller and newer money, in the same
line of work, and it is a going concern rather than a rival: CIN sponsors a hero
team of its own and does not pretend otherwise.

## H-Day and After

New Haven is what H-Day built.

Banking, insurance and corporate headquarters left the old district during the
reconstruction, and the city and the displaced firms between them decided not to
put any of it back. The site north of the harbor was assembled, raised and
opened for construction inside four years. The first towers took occupancy in
2029 and the district was substantially complete by 2033.

The decision has never been formally defended, because it never had to be. Firms
that had watched a virus released over their own street chose not to return to
that street, which is a preference nobody in city government was inclined to
argue with.

## Who's There

Around 60,000 people work in New Haven on a weekday. Rather fewer than 9,000
live here, in the apartment towers along the northern edge, and the ratio is the
single most-cited fact about the district. It empties in the evening.

Pantheon lives at the top of Olympus Tower, or is housed there. Not every member
uses the accommodation and Arkon has never claimed otherwise. The heroes are the
most-photographed residents of the district by a wide margin, and their presence
is the reason a good deal of the foot traffic here is people hoping to see
something.

The rest of the working population is corporate and professional: finance,
insurance, media, law, and the service businesses that exist because those
people need lunch.

## The Public Record

The engineering is documented in detail. Fill volumes, drainage capacity, pump
specifications and the finished grade are all public, and were published as the
project went up rather than afterwards.

Occupancy, assessed values and the tax abatements offered to the firms that
relocated are on record with the city. The abatements were substantial, they
were granted openly, and they are the subject of a recurring argument at budget
time that has never changed anybody's vote.

Arkon Media is a public company and files accordingly. What Pantheon costs to
operate appears in those filings, folded into a line item that does not separate
it out.

## Contested / unconfirmed

**Whether New Haven is a district or a campus.** It has a civic league, on
paper. The league's meetings are attended in single figures, which the city
treats as a district that has not organised itself and which residents of other
districts treat as evidence that nobody here regards the place as somewhere they
are from.

**What the district cost the rest of the city.** New Haven was raised at the
same time the city was arguing about whether it could afford a floodwall at all,
and the money came from different places. Whether it could have come from the
same place, and gone elsewhere, is asked at every hearing on Harborworks
phasing. The city's answer has been consistent and has convinced nobody who was
not already convinced.
