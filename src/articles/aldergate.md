Aldergate occupies the west bank opposite the [[The Old Financial District|Old
Financial District]], on the rise above the river, and it is where the people
who did well out of [[Dawnbreak City]] have lived since there was a city to do
well out of. The courts are here. So are the older hospitals, the older schools
and the old-guard end of the police department.

## The Place

Brick and stone, deep front gardens, and street trees planted in the twenties
that are now the largest living things in the city. The plan here is the
founders' plan executed properly: wide avenues on the ridge line, narrower
streets stepping down toward the water, and a park at the top that was drawn
before any of the houses were.

The housing is generous and old, and a good deal of it is in the hands of the
third or fourth generation of the same families. Where it has changed hands it
has usually gone to a hospital consultant, a partner in a firm, or somebody from
[[New Haven]] who wanted an address with a past attached.

### The institutions

Aldergate holds the parts of Dawnbreak that predate H-Day and intend to outlast
whatever comes next.

**St. Alder's** is the older of the city's two general hospitals, opened in 1927
and rebuilt around its own original block twice. **The Whitlock School** and the
**Aldergate Free School** are the city's oldest, the first fee-paying and the
second not, founded within a year of each other by people who disagreed about
which kind Dawnbreak needed and settled it by building both. The county-style
courthouse at the top of Court Street is where the city's serious business is
heard, and the **Dawnbreak City Police** keeps its headquarters two streets from
it, which is the arrangement the founders drew and nobody has revisited.

The district's civic league is the oldest in the city and has never needed to be
loud. Its membership overlaps considerably with the boards of the institutions
above, which is a fact its critics raise and which the league does not dispute.

### High ground

Aldergate sits on the ridge and does not flood. The lower streets nearest the
river take water at their bottom ends in a bad autumn and the rest of the
district does not, and the Harborworks wall does not run here because it does not
need to.

This is stated locally as a matter of geology rather than of luck, which is true.
It is also the reason Aldergate has been the quietest district in the city's
long argument about the wall. Nobody here is outside a line, and nobody here is
inside one either.

## H-Day and After

Aldergate is directly across the water from the release. The district was not
under it and the physical effects here were limited, but it was near enough that
the day is a first-hand memory rather than a broadcast one for everybody who was
in the district that morning.

**St. Alder's took the casualties.** It was the closest hospital to the
financial district that was not in the financial district, and for eleven days it
ran past every capacity it had. The hospital's own account of that period is
published, unsparing, and is the single most useful public document about what
H-Day did to people rather than to buildings.

The institutional cost was heavy and specific. Hospital staff were affected at
rates comparable to the fire companies of [[Grid Row]], for the same reason. The
police department lost officers. Both organisations have their 2028 rolls on
public display in their own buildings, which was not coordinated between them.

Aldergate itself did not change much afterwards. The families stayed, the
property values held, and the district's institutions carried a great deal of the
city's recovery without the district itself being altered by it. This is
occasionally raised as a criticism and is more accurately a description.

## Who's There

About 24,000 residents. Older, wealthier and more settled than the city average,
with the lowest turnover in Dawnbreak after Grid Row, and for the opposite
reason.

A large professional working population comes in daily to the hospital, the
courts, the schools and the law firms along Court Street. The domestic and
service employment attached to the residential streets is substantial and is
mostly held by people who live in other districts.

## The Public Record

The founders' plan for Aldergate survives in the city archive, including the park
and the avenue widths, and the district has been built out almost exactly as
drawn.

St. Alder's publishes its H-Day account and its 2028 staff losses. The police
department publishes the equivalent. The institutions' founding documents,
charters and board minutes are largely public, and the overlap between those
boards and the civic league's membership is a matter anybody can establish from
them.
