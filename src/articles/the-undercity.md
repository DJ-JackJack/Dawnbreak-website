The Undercity is what [[Dawnbreak City]] calls the space under itself, and the
name is local rather than official. In municipal documents it does not exist as
a single thing. It appears as storm drainage, as the sewer interceptors, as duct
runs, as the terminal works, as service galleries, and as a hundred years of
capital projects that each tied into whatever was already down there.

## The Place

Dawnbreak was platted whole in 1921 and its first utilities were laid as one
system rather than accumulated street by street, which is the reason the space
beneath it is continuous enough to have earned a name. Everything built
afterwards connected to that system. Nothing has ever replaced it.

What is down there, in rough order of age: the founders' brick interceptors and
storm culverts, large enough to walk in because that was how they were
maintained; the 1920s freight and coal galleries under [[Grid Row]], which are
the largest single works and the reason the network is denser there than
anywhere; sixty years of power and telephone duct; the Harbor Tunnel's service
bores; and, most recently and most extensively, the Harborworks pump galleries,
culverts and gate machinery put in during the 2030s.

That last layer matters more than its age suggests. The city's defence against
the sea is very largely underground, and a great deal of what the Undercity now
consists of was built in the last decade to move water.

### Where it runs

Under everything, unevenly.

The network is densest beneath Grid Row, where the power, freight and drainage
works all converge, and beneath the [[The Old Financial District|Old Financial
District]], where the oldest and largest brick culverts run out to the harbor.
[[New Haven]] has the newest and tidiest below-grade works in the city, laid in
one piece with the made ground above them. [[Fenmoor Flats]] has almost nothing,
because you cannot tunnel usefully in a marsh, and what the Flats have instead is
a field drainage system that the city does not consider part of the same thing at
all.

### What the city says about it

The city's position has been consistent for decades. The Undercity is utilities.
Access points are locked and monitored, entry is restricted to utility crews and
contractors, and no comprehensive map is published, on the stated grounds that a
map of every way into the city's water, power and drainage would be useful to
people the city would rather not assist.

The maps exist. Public works holds them, engineers work from them, and the refusal
is to publication rather than to their existence.

### Who goes down there

Utility crews, drainage inspectors and the contractors working the Harborworks
galleries account for nearly all authorised entry.

Beyond that, the answer is less tidy. The culverts are dry for most of the year,
they hold their temperature, and people with nowhere else to be have slept in
them every winter the city has existed. Outreach services know this, work on the
basis that it is happening, and do not discuss the specific locations, on the
reasoning that publishing them would close them.

The network also has an older reputation as the way to move around Dawnbreak
without being seen, which is not a claim anybody has had to prove: a continuous
walkable system with unpublished entrances runs under a city of a quarter of a
million people, and what follows from that follows without help. Residents of
Grid Row describe more traffic beneath their streets than a utility system should
generate. No photograph of any of it has ever been produced.

## H-Day and After

Nothing was released underground and the Undercity recorded no effects on
11 June 2028.

It was used heavily in the weeks afterwards. The culverts under the financial
district were among the few routes into the affected blocks that did not require
crossing controlled ground above, and utility crews worked them continuously to
keep water and power on in a district everybody else had left.

The Harborworks programme changed the Undercity more than H-Day did. Between 2031
and the present, the city put more below-grade volume into the ground than in the
preceding fifty years, and it did so under the districts inside the wall. The
network's shape now reflects the wall's alignment, which means the newest and
best-built parts of the Undercity run under the districts that were already
being protected.

## Who's There

Nobody lives here in any sense the city recognises, and some people do.

The working population is small: perhaps 60 municipal staff and contractors with
routine below-grade duties, plus the Harborworks crews, whose numbers rise and
fall with the phase.

## The Public Record

Individual systems are documented in detail. Sewer and storm drainage as-builts,
duct records, the terminal works drawings and the complete Harborworks
engineering are all held by the city, and most of it is available on request, one
system at a time.

What does not exist publicly is the composite. No single document shows the whole
network, and the city has declined to assemble one on the record at least four
times since 1994, giving the same reason each time.
