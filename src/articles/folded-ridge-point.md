Folded Ridge Point is the low southern point beyond [[The Harbor]], reached by
the Harbor Tunnel, and most of it belongs to the federal government. The runway
and the helipads are here, along with the Coast Guard station, and the fence
around them accounts for a majority of the district's land area. It is the least
populated district in [[Dawnbreak City]] and the only one the city does not
entirely administer.

## The Place

Flat, exposed and mostly paving. The point is a spit of made and natural ground
running out between the channel and the open water, and the wind across it is
the first thing anybody notices.

The single runway runs the length of the point on a northeast alignment, with
the apron, the hangars and the helipads on the sheltered side. Everything else is
fence, approach lighting and the road in from the tunnel. At night the district
is visible from most of the harbor frontage as a long line of white and red
across the water, which is how the majority of the city's residents have ever
seen it.

### The federal ground

The airfield was built in 1943, the city annexed the point the same year, and
neither of those things happened because Dawnbreak wanted them. The land was
taken because it was flat, empty and next to deep water, and the city was given
the civil administration of a district that was already spoken for.

Federal aviation still uses it. The Coast Guard station runs search and rescue
and fisheries patrols from the point and is the most visible of the tenants, with
its own pier on the sheltered side. Agency aircraft come and go on business the
district is not told about, including the
[[Department of Metahuman Damage Mitigation]], whose flights account for a
noticeable share of the movements and none of the published schedule.

**Dawnbreak is not a Navy town.** There is no fleet here and there never has
been. The grey aircraft on the apron belong to agencies rather than to a service,
which is a distinction the city has had to make often enough that the harbor
tour boats now make it on their behalf.

### What the city keeps

The civilian remainder is a strip along the tunnel road: a haulage depot, two
marine engineering firms, the fuel terminal, a diner that has outlived four
owners, and about 900 people living in the small grid of streets behind them,
which is the whole of the district's residential population.

## H-Day and After

Nothing was released here and nothing reached the point. The district recorded no
effects on 11 June 2028.

What the point did was receive. Federal aircraft began arriving on the afternoon
of the 11th and did not stop for three weeks, and every federal person who came
to Dawnbreak in the response came through this runway. The tunnel road was
controlled for eleven days. Residents of the civilian strip needed a pass to
reach their own houses, which was resolved without ceremony and is still
resented.

The federal presence never went back to its 2028 level. Movements settled at
roughly double what they had been, the DMDM established its aviation use in the
years afterward, and the fence line moved outward twice, in 2031 and 2036, taking
the old approach road and a marine yard with it.

## Who's There

Around 900 residents, and a working population that the district cannot count
because most of it works behind the fence and is not obliged to say so.

The civilian side is industrial and marine: haulage, engineering, fuel handling,
and the trades that service them. Coast Guard families make up a share of the
residential strip and turn over on posting cycles, which gives the district a
population that partly refreshes every few years and partly has not moved in
generations.

The civic league is small and its agenda is almost entirely about the fence: the
road, the noise, the approach lighting, and the two land transfers. It has never
had a federal representative attend a meeting.

## The Public Record

The 1943 annexation and the original land taking are documented, as are the 1931
and 1936 boundary changes, though the second of those is recorded only as
acreage.

Civil aviation movements are published. Federal movements are not. The Coast
Guard publishes its search and rescue statistics annually and they are the most
detailed public information about anything that happens on the point.

Noise complaints to the city are logged and the log is public. The city forwards
them and has no authority to act on them, a position it states plainly in its
own response letters.
