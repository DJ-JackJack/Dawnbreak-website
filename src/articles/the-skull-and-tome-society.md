The Skull and Tome Society is an order of magic users whose purpose is to keep
everybody else from finding out that magic is real. It holds no legal authority
of any kind, anywhere. It is nonetheless the most consequential institution in
the magical world, and by its own measure it has been losing ground since June
11, 2028.

## Founding

The Society does not publish its history and the date it began is not recorded.

What it was founded to do has never been in question, because it is stated as a
law rather than as a mission, and it is the same law now that it has always
been. Magic must be protected from the masses. Only those naturally gifted with
the ability should know that it exists.

Everything the order does follows from those two sentences, including the parts
of it that people find hard to forgive.

## Structure

Most major cities have a branch office. The branches are the Society's presence
in the world and they exist, more than anything else, to put a door somewhere.

What the door opens onto is the **Eternal Library**, a demiplane the Society
maintains as its hub. Communication, research and organization happen there
rather than in any branch, which means a member in one city and a member in
another are working in the same building whenever it matters. Distance is an
administrative detail for this organization in a way it is not for any other.

The Library is also the hoard. What the Society has accumulated there is the
magical knowledge of the world, gathered over a span nobody outside it can put a
number to, and it is the reason the rest of this article is possible to write.

## Operations

The Society has no authority over magic users and has never claimed any. No
charter grants it jurisdiction. No government recognises it. A practitioner who
disregards the Society entirely breaks no law and can be told so by any lawyer
they care to ask.

It is obeyed anyway, and the reason is the Library. A practitioner who wants to
learn anything past what they can work out unaided needs access to what the
Society holds, and the Society decides who gets it. That is the whole mechanism
and it is enough. An order that controls what can be learned has no particular
need for the power to compel.

The preference is for peaceful resolution and most of the time that is what
happens. Someone talks to someone. An arrangement is reached. The person who was
becoming a problem stops being one, and the Society considers the matter closed
and the knowledge still worth extending to them.

### The Black Guard

The rest of the time there is the Black Guard, which is the Society's
enforcement arm. It is small, it is rarely deployed, and neither of those is a
weakness.

What it actually does is largely a matter of stories. Every prospective witch,
wizard, sorcerer and charmcaster hears at least one, usually early, usually from
whoever is teaching them. Somebody crossed a line. Somebody was dealt with.

The stories are the enforcement. A body that holds the knowledge everyone needs,
answers to no court, and has a reputation every apprentice acquires before they
acquire anything useful does not have to send people very often.

## In the City

Dawnbreak is where the strange stopped being deniable.

BEV-131 was not the first sign that the world held more than the public
believed. It was the event that made it undeniable and put it on every screen in
the country at once, and for an order whose entire function is deniability,
[[H-Day]] is not primarily a tragedy. It is the day the work got enormously
harder, and it has not got easier in the twelve years since.

The complication that followed is heroes. Some of them publicly claim magical
powers, in front of cameras, as a matter of professional identity. The Society
spent however long it has existed making sure no one could point at a
practitioner, and Dawnbreak now has practitioners with merchandise.

[[Witchmark]] is the sharpest version of that problem anywhere and she is in
this city. She is an adept magician, she is field commander of the most watched
hero team in the country, and her employer owns the news.

## The Public Record

There is none, in this city or any other.

No government recognises the Society. No filing names it. No journalist has
published on it. Across the whole run of Dawnbreak's newspapers, its broadcast
years and whatever [[The Dawnbreak Trumpet]] currently is, the Skull and Tome
Society has left no mark on the public record of this city or any other it
operates in.

Whatever else is true of the order, it remains extremely good at the one thing
it does.
