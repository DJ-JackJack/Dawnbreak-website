Tidewater Commons holds the ground south of [[Aldergate]], between the river and
the southern highway, and it is where [[Dawnbreak City]] does the things it does
together. The stadium is here, the arena is here, and so are the courts, the main
library, the civic auditorium and the fairground. It is also the lowest occupied
ground on the west bank, which is the fact everything else about the district
eventually runs into.

## The Place

Open ground, and a great deal of it. The founders set this land aside in 1923 as
parkland and fairground, on the reasonable grounds that it flooded and could not
be built on profitably, and the city has spent a century filling it with the
kinds of buildings that need space around them rather than tenants above them.

The result is a district with almost no street wall. Playing fields run into car
parks, car parks run into the fairground, and the big buildings sit in the middle
of their own ground with room to walk around them. On an ordinary weekday
afternoon it is the emptiest part of the city. On a Saturday in season it holds
more people than any other district.

### What is here

The stadium takes 34,000 and is used for football, for the summer concert series
and for the graduations of every public high school in the city, which happen
over four consecutive evenings each June and are the largest civic event
Dawnbreak holds that nobody outside the city has heard of.

The arena is smaller, newer, and in use most nights. The courts and the civic
auditorium sit at the northern edge where the ground rises slightly toward
Aldergate. The fairground has been a fairground since before the city bought it.

### The water

The Commons floods. It has always flooded, it was set aside because it flooded,
and the low fields go under several times a year without anybody treating it as
an event. Ground crews have a routine for it. The pitch drains are sized for it.
The fixture list is built around the autumn tides and has been for decades.

The Harborworks floodwall turns inland at the district's boundary. Tidewater
Commons is therefore outside the line, and the city's position is that this is
correct: the district is designed to flood, the buildings that matter sit on the
higher northern edge, and defending open parkland would cost a great deal to
protect grass.

The counter-argument is not about the grass. Water that lands on the Commons has
to go somewhere, the fields drain north and west into streets that were not
designed to take it, and the district's civic league has spent six years asking
for the drainage study that would settle whether the wall's alignment made that
worse. The study has been scheduled twice.

## H-Day and After

The Commons was not touched by the release. It is well upriver and across the
water from the [[The Old Financial District|financial district]], and no part of
the district recorded any physical effect.

What it did was take people. The stadium was the city's primary reception point
in the days afterward, because it was the only structure in Dawnbreak that could
hold a crowd, feed it and count it at the same time. The concourse was a triage
floor for nine days. The car parks held the federal vehicles. For most of a
fortnight the Commons was the place people went to look for someone.

The city has never marked this and the district has never asked it to. The
stadium's June graduations continued that year on the original dates, three weeks
after the concourse cleared, which was a decision taken by the school board
rather than by anybody in city government and which is remembered locally as the
correct one.

## Who's There

Around 9,000 residents, most of them in the older terraced streets along the
northern edge where the ground is high enough to build on, and a working
population that swings enormously with the fixture list.

The permanent institutions employ most of the rest: the courts, the library, the
schools, the grounds crews and the trades who keep the venues running. The
district's civic league is well attended and unusually specific in what it asks
for, which is a consequence of having one clear grievance and a drainage map.

## The Public Record

Attendance, bookings and venue revenues are published by the city. Flood records
for the Commons run back to 1954 with the rest of the city's tide data, and the
fields' inundation days are logged separately by the parks department, which has
kept that count since 1961.

The Harborworks alignment and the reasoning for turning inland here are public.
The drainage study is not, because it has not been carried out.
