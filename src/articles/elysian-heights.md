Elysian Heights covers the hills north of [[New Haven]], and it is the newest
residential district in [[Dawnbreak City]] and the highest ground in it. The
subdivision was laid out in 2032 for the people who came with the corporate
rebuild, and it sits about forty feet above a harbor whose water level is the
central municipal argument of the age.

## The Place

Winding streets, large lots, mature planting brought in at size, and a golf
course the developers built first because the lots facing it sold for more. The
houses are new, large and varied in the way a design code produces: no two
identical, none of them surprising.

There is no through traffic. The road pattern was drawn to prevent it, with three
entrances off the New Haven side and nothing crossing the district at all, and
the effect is that almost nobody in Dawnbreak has been to Elysian Heights unless
they had a reason.

### Where the name came from

The subdivision was named by its developers, who were reaching for the same
register [[Arkon Media]] had already stamped on the city with Olympus Tower and
[[Pantheon]]. Elysium is the part of the Greek afterlife reserved for heroes.

Nobody involved appears to have thought this through, and the name has been in
use long enough that pointing it out is now the mark of somebody who has just
noticed. It is a district for the living, named for where the honoured dead
go, sold on the strength of the association.

### Above the water

The Heights do not flood and cannot. This is the only district in the city where
the tide is not a subject.

That has made it the quietest participant in the Harborworks argument and the
most frequently cited example in it. Residents of the deferred districts observe
that the newest money in Dawnbreak bought the only ground the sea will never
reach, which is true and is not something the district's residents chose or
arranged. The developers chose it. The buyers bought a view.

## H-Day and After

Elysian Heights did not exist on 11 June 2028. The hills were undeveloped, and
the district records no relation to H-Day beyond having been built four years
afterwards on the strength of it.

This is the sharpest version of a pattern the city has elsewhere. New Haven was
built to receive business displaced by the release; the Heights were built to
house the people who came with that business. Both districts are direct products
of the worst day in Dawnbreak's history, and neither of them experienced it.
Residents who arrived after 2032 have no first-hand memory of the event that made
their neighbourhood, and the district has no institutions old enough to carry one
for them.

## Who's There

Around 11,000 residents in roughly 3,800 households, which is the largest average
household size and the smallest population density in the city at the same time.

The working population is executive, professional and medical, commuting the
short distance down into New Haven or across to [[Aldergate]]. A significant
service and maintenance workforce comes up daily from [[Grid Row]] and
[[Fenmoor Flats]] to run the grounds, the houses and the golf course, and none of
it lives here.

The civic league is well funded, well attended and almost entirely concerned with
the district itself: planting, the covenant, the entrances, the golf course's
water use. It is effective on those subjects and has taken no position on the
floodwall.

## The Public Record

The subdivision plat, the design covenant and the developers' filings are all on
record with the city, including the sales material, which is where the naming
turns up in writing for the first time.

Assessed values here are the highest in Dawnbreak and are published like anybody
else's. The golf course's water abstraction is licensed and the licence is
public, which is the one municipal subject on which the Heights and the districts
below it have ever exchanged words in a hearing room.
