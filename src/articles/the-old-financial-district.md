The district where H-Day happened. Twelve years on it is fully rebuilt, fully
occupied and fully functional, and it is still the cheapest real estate in
[[Dawnbreak City]]. No amount of construction has closed the gap between what
the place is and what people are willing to believe about it.

## The Place

Glass and rebuilt stone on a street plan older than anything around it. There
was a wharf and a handful of lanes at the mouth of the Nashoke before the
Dawnbreak Company bought the land, and when the founders platted the city in
1921 they laid their grid around what was already there rather than through it.
The oldest blocks in the district still follow those lanes. The reconstruction
after H-Day kept the original footprint as well, rather than taking the chance to
redraw it, so the district holds its narrow canyon streets with new towers
dropped into old outlines.

It is quieter than a financial district should be. Far fewer people work here
than the buildings were designed to hold, and at street level that reads as
space: room on the pavement, tables free at lunchtime, a bus that arrives with
seats on it. Residents describe this as the best thing about living here.
Visitors tend to describe it as eerie, which residents find tiresome.

### The oldest and lowest ground

The district sits on the east bank at the harbor turn, on the lowest built
ground in the city. It has always taken water. The streets nearest the frontage
go under on a spring tide and the edges flood most autumns, and both facts long
predate anybody's interest in the subject.

It is also inside the Harborworks floodwall, and has been since the programme
began in 2031. This is one of the three areas the wall was drawn to protect, and
the protection is real. The 2036 surge reached the district's low edge and went
no further, which is the first time in the city's history that sentence could be
written about this ground.

It moved nothing. Owners who expected a floodwall to lift values were
disappointed, and assessments here have tracked the same flat line through eight
years of wall-building. Whatever is wrong with the district in the public mind,
it was never the water.

### What the buildings hold

Office occupancy is stable and has been for years, which is not the same as
being full. The towers were built for a working population several times the one
they have, and a good deal of the district's floor space is let to tenants who
need an address more than they need a floor.

## H-Day and After

On **11 June 2028**, [[Dr. Erroghastor]] released **BEV-131** over this
district. Everything the city now calls H-Day began in the airspace above these
blocks, and the people directly beneath it were the first affected anywhere.

Reconstruction was fast and well funded. The district was structurally whole
again inside a decade and by every measurable standard it recovered.

What did not return was its function. The corporate and financial business that
gave the district its name relocated during the rebuild and never came back,
consolidating instead into [[New Haven]], which was built to receive it on
raised ground across the harbor and has held that role since. The Old Financial
District kept the name and lost the work.

Harborworks was authorised three years after the rebuild finished. City engineers
had been asking for a floodwall since the 1990s and had been refused on cost
every time, and the alignment that finally passed runs around a district the city
had just spent a decade rebuilding. The engineering case for protecting this
ground is old and sound. The timing is its own argument, and it is made at every
hearing by people who live outside the line.

## Who's There

Two populations, with very little overlap.

The first is residential: people for whom the rent is the deciding factor.
Cheapest in the city, in a district with functioning transit, intact
infrastructure and a floodwall. For a household with no other option this is the
option, and for a household with options it is frequently a very good deal that
they decline for reasons they do not always articulate.

The second is corporate, and specifically the kind of corporate presence that
values not being asked about. Shell companies and black-budget contractors hold a
disproportionate share of the district's office space. The attraction is
understood to be the same one that keeps the rents low: nobody here is curious
about anybody else.

## The Public Record

The district's recovery is a matter of public record and is not disputed. The
rebuild completed, the buildings are safe, occupancy is stable. City
communications have reported this consistently and accurately for over a decade.

Property values have not followed. The gap between the district's documented
condition and its market value is the most legible fact about the place, and no
explanation the city has offered for it has satisfied anyone.

Tide records for this frontage run back to 1954. The Harborworks alignment is
public, as are the assessments that have not moved since it went up.

Mutation cases still surface among former residents and workers, rarely and at
intervals. They have never been proven and never been debunked. Individually the
cases are plausible. Collectively they remain unverified, no study has settled
the question, and the only official position is that BEV-131 is not considered to
remain active in the environment. Residents generally do not discuss it. People
who do not live here discuss it constantly.
