Cape Crooked is the barrier spit that runs northeast off the end of
[[Grid Row]], with the Atlantic on one side and the sheltered water on the
other. It was a beach resort with its own post office and its own opinion of
itself before [[Dawnbreak City]] annexed it in 1931, and it is the oldest
continuously settled ground in the city by roughly thirty years.

## The Place

A single road runs the length of the spit with the houses on either side of it,
and at no point is the ocean more than a few hundred yards away. Ocean beach to
the east, piers and small-boat moorings on the sheltered side, and cottages in
between: weathered, close together, raised on posts where the owners have got
round to it.

The building stock is the oldest in Dawnbreak and looks it. Cape Crooked went up
between 1889 and the First World War as a summer place, in timber, and a
surprising amount of that timber is still standing because it has been repaired
continuously for a hundred and thirty years by people who knew they would have
to do it again.

### A resort that outlived the resort trade

The Crooked Pier Hotel closed in 1958 and burned in 1961. The bathing pavilions
went in the sixties. The carousel ran until 1974 and its building is still there,
used for storage, with the ceiling paint intact.

What replaced the resort was the thing resorts turn into when the excursion
trains stop: a year-round district of people living in buildings designed for
three months' use. Roughly two thirds of the housing is now permanently
occupied. The rest is still seasonal, and the summer population is about triple
the winter one.

### The water on both sides

The spit is sand. It has moved within living memory, it loses beach at the
northern end and gains it at the southern, and every serious storm since
annexation has taken houses off it.

Cape Crooked is outside the Harborworks floodwall and always was. There is no
alignment on which a harbor wall protects a barrier island a mile out from the
harbor, and the district knows it: what the Flats and the Commons argue about is
phasing, and what Cape Crooked argues about is whether anything is coming at all.
The programme's later phase for this district is a dune and revetment scheme
rather than a wall, it has never been funded beyond design, and the design is
eleven years old.

Insurance here is a subject people do not raise socially.

## H-Day and After

Cape Crooked recorded no effects on 11 June 2028. It is well east of the
release, separated from it by the whole width of the harbor, and the wind that
morning carried away from the coast rather than along it.

The district's H-Day is a story about the day after. The spit's small-boat fleet
went across the harbor on the 12th and spent the following week moving people
and supplies, because the road bridges were controlled and the boats were not.
Nobody organised it. There is no record of who went, since no list was kept, and
the district has been mildly annoyed about the lack of a record for twelve years.

The population rose slightly afterwards, in the same movement that took
households west to [[Fenmoor Flats]]: cheap housing, a long way from where it
happened.

## Who's There

About 6,400 permanent residents, tripling in July and August. The permanent
population is a mix of families who have been on the spit for generations, people
who arrived because it was cheap, and a smaller number who came for the water and
stayed.

Work is seasonal and thin. The marina, the boatyard, the summer trade, the two
schools and the fire station account for most local employment, and a good share
of the district commutes into Grid Row or across to the port.

The civic league is old, informal and effective within a very narrow range: it is
extremely good at getting the road cleared and the drains dug, and has had no
success whatsoever on the revetment.

## The Public Record

The annexation is documented, including the 1931 vote, which passed on the spit
by a margin of nineteen and is still occasionally mentioned.

Shoreline surveys have been run annually since 1954 and show the spit's movement
plainly. Storm losses are recorded property by property. The revetment design and
its costings are public, as is every budget cycle in which it has not appeared.
