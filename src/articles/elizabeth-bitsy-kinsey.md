Elizabeth Kinsey has been Lead Coroner of Dawnbreak City since 2030, and on
the staff of the coroner's office for twenty years before that. She is
fifty-six. She has established the cause of death for more metahumans than
anyone living, most of them in a city that had no idea what it was looking at,
and she will tell anyone what she found in the order she found it.

## Background

She is from Rushville, Nebraska, a town of about eight hundred in the
northwest corner of the state, where the hospital that could do more than
stabilise a person was three hours off and everyone understood the arithmetic
of that. People there say what they mean, because the alternative takes longer
and the town finds out regardless. She brought the habit to a city of two
million and left it exactly as it was, and the city has largely adjusted around
her.

Everyone calls her Bitsy. She has answered to it since she was four, she gives
the name that way on the stand, and it has cost three defence counsel a line of
questioning they thought they had.

She took the work in her twenties for the ordinary reason people take it: a job
in a hospital basement that others passed over, and a knack for it that
surprised her. Dawnbreak in the 2000s buried its dead the way any city does.
The office signed out traffic, industry, overdoses, and a great many old people
who died at home on a Sunday. She expected thirty-five years of that and a
pension.

The office sits at the bottom of Court Street in [[Aldergate]], four minutes
from the courthouse and the police headquarters, where the founders put all
three in the 1920s and where all three have stayed. Dawnbreak is an independent
city inside no county, so the difficult cases stay with her and the nearest
second opinion is in Richmond. That geography has shaped her career more than
any decision she made about it.

## June 11, 2028

She was forty-four, a senior investigator, and nine streets from the
[[The Old Financial District|Old Financial District]] when it started. She
stayed in the building for nine days.

What came in that week fell outside everything the staff had been trained on,
and every office she might have called was in the same position. Exposure ran
longest for the people who stayed to pull others out, so a great many of the
bodies were first responders, and in a city this size a great many of those
were people the staff knew by name. The office lost three of its own. Bitsy did
the work on two of them herself, because asking anyone else was worse.

Somewhere in that fortnight she began keeping notes on what she was seeing. The
case files were one thing; this was a separate running record of what the virus
did to tissue, started because by about the third day it was clear that someone
had to and that it would matter. She has kept it since. That notebook, long
outgrown, is the closest thing the state has to a reference text on metahuman
physiology under stress.

Asked about 2028, she answers, in the same flat and complete way she answers
everything, and people who expected a story about grief generally wish they had
left it alone.

## The Work

Most of what crosses her table is sad and entirely explicable, and always has
been. The office signs out several hundred deaths a year that have nothing to
do with any of this.

The rest is why people outside Dawnbreak know her name. The cause of death is
sometimes a power, and in a good number of those cases the only person in the
state who has seen that power do that to a body is the one standing in the
room. A man whose bone density trebled across four years. A woman killed by
something other than the fall she took. She has written findings the literature
is silent on, and defended every one of them in court on the strength of the
notes underneath.

Her working relationship with the [[Dawnbreak City Police]] is close, constant
and unsentimental. The Metahuman Containment Unit sends her its fatalities and
takes her conclusions seriously, which it reserves for a short list of experts,
and the reason is that her findings hold in both directions. She has told the
MCU that a death it wanted to be a metahuman death was a heart attack, and said
it in the room, to the officers who wanted the other answer.

## In the City

The bluntness is the first thing anyone reports about her and the thing most
often misread. She tells a detective, a widow, a council member or a hero
exactly what she found, in the order she found it, entire. She treats this as
procedure rather than character. People given bad news slowly, in stages, by
someone else deciding what they can handle, tend to say afterwards that they
hated it, and she has spared everyone that from the beginning.

She is also, and this surprises people who have only met her professionally,
kind. She makes the call to the family herself rather than sending it down the
hall. She answers the question that was actually asked. She spares the
performance of sympathy on people in no condition to appreciate it, and she
lets them take their time. The kindness and the bluntness are one thing in her,
and she is puzzled when they are treated as opposites.

## The Public Record

Her findings are public once a case closes, and a notable number of Dawnbreak's
are worded with a precision that stops exactly where the record stops.

She has testified more than four hundred times. The transcripts are public,
every finding of hers has survived cross-examination, and a fair number of
local attorneys can quote the 2036 exchange in which she was asked whether she
was certain and replied that she was paid to be right rather than certain, and
that on this occasion she was both.

The office's internal reference on metahuman physiology stays unpublished. More
than one body with the standing to insist has asked for it. It remains where it
is.
