The Harbor is the reason [[Dawnbreak City]] is here. The Nashoke turns south at
this point and the channel runs deep enough to take ocean freight without
dredging, which is the fact the founders bought land on. The district is the
water, the frontage on both sides of it, and the working ground behind the
piers.

## The Place

Container cranes on the east side, bulk piers and the tank farm on the west, a
dry dock, a tug basin, and a customs house that has been rebuilt twice on the
same foundation. Behind all of it, warehousing and haulage yards and the road
system that serves them, which is the only part of the district a visitor is
likely to drive through.

Very little of this is pretty and none of it was meant to be. The Harbor is
zoned and built for moving weight.

### The frontage and the wall

The Harborworks floodwall's centrepiece runs along this frontage, and the
district is therefore the best-defended ground in the city against the thing the
city is actually threatened by. The wall carries the raised roadway on its
inland face and ties into pumping stations at three points. During a surge the
gates close and the port stops working, which has happened four times since the
wall went up.

The engineering is not controversial here. What it means for everybody outside
it is argued elsewhere, at length.

### Who works it

Around 4,000 people work in the district directly and several times that number
depend on it. The longshore local is the oldest continuous union in the city and
predates the terminal it works. Pilots take ships in and out on a rota that has
barely changed in a century. Tug crews, riggers, customs officers, warehouse
labour, and the tradesmen who keep the cranes running make up most of the rest.

The Harbor Tunnel carries the southern highway under the channel to
[[Folded Ridge Point]]. Freight moves in and out of the district by rail through
[[Grid Row]] and by truck through everywhere else, which is why the roads here
are in the condition they are in.

## H-Day and After

The port did not stop.

The release was over the [[The Old Financial District|financial district]] and
the harbor is immediately downriver of it. Crews on the water that morning were
close enough to see it happen. What the district lost was people rather than
capacity, in a pattern common across the city: the men and women who went toward
the thing were affected at rates far above those who went away from it, and the
longshore local's rolls for 2028 record it plainly.

Cargo movements for that year fell by less than a fifth and recovered inside
eighteen months. This is the fact the district leads with when it is asked about
H-Day, and it is offered less as pride than as a statement about what a port is.

## Who's There

Almost nobody lives here. The residential population is under 400, concentrated
in the old block behind the customs house, and it has been falling for fifty
years as the working ground has spread.

The daytime district is entirely working population. There is a seafarers'
mission on Cutwater Street, which has been open since 1931 and provides beds,
meals and telephones to crews between ships, and which is one of the few places
in Dawnbreak where a person can turn up with no documents and be given a
sandwich rather than a question.

## The Public Record

Tonnage, vessel calls and berth occupancy are published monthly. The customs
service publishes seizure statistics annually. The floodwall's gate closures are
logged and the log is public, as are the four dates on which the port has shut.

Employment figures are collected by the port authority and by the union
separately, and the two sets do not quite agree, which both organisations
attribute to how casual shifts are counted.

## Contested / unconfirmed

**The unofficial traffic.** The Harbor is widely held to be the easiest way into
Dawnbreak for a person or an object that would rather not be recorded arriving.
Customs holds that the port is controlled, that its seizure rate is comparable
to any east coast facility of the size, and that the reputation is folklore.
People who work the district treat the reputation as a straightforward
description of what a working port is, and do not regard saying so as an
accusation against anybody in particular.

Both accounts have been consistent for decades. No inquiry has been held,
because no specific allegation has ever been made that would require one.

**What comes through for the metahuman trade.** It is assumed in several
quarters that material of interest to people with abilities moves through this
district, and the assumption is old enough to have hardened into something
people state as fact. Nothing has been demonstrated. The seizure record shows
the ordinary run of a port and nothing that would support the stronger version
of the claim.
