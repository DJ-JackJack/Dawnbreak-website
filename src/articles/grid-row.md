Grid Row runs northeast from the harbor along the rail line, and it is named for
the electrical grid rather than for its street pattern. People get this wrong
constantly. The streets here are the least gridded in [[Dawnbreak City]],
because they were laid to serve a rail terminal and bend wherever the track
bends.

## The Place

Everything the city runs on arrives or is made here. The generating station
stands at the water end, the substations step down from it in a line inland, and
the rail yards and freight gantries fill the ground between the track and the
river. Power, freight and drainage all converge on this one district, and none
of it was put here for the people who live here. It was put here because the
water was deep, the land was cheap, and the track had to end somewhere.

The housing came second and was built for the men working the terminal. Brick
row houses, two and three storeys, tight to the street, with the yards behind
them. Most of it is still standing and most of it is still lived in, in many
cases by people descended from the households it was built for.

### What the district carries

Dawnbreak's electricity comes through Grid Row. So does most of what the city
eats, wears and builds with, by rail into the terminal and out again by truck.
The station and the yards run continuously and have since 1926. This is the
oldest continuously working part of the city, older in that sense than the
financial district it helped pay for, and the district takes the point
seriously.

What Grid Row gets back for it is an argument that has been running for ninety
years. The generating station is inside the Harborworks floodwall. So are the
substations and the freight terminal. The route the wall takes through the
district follows that infrastructure rather than the streets, so a good deal of
Grid Row housing sits on the wet side of a wall built through Grid Row. The city
notes, correctly, that a flooded substation puts the whole city dark. Residents
note that the wall could have gone around both.

### The institutions

The people who arrived here in the 1920s built their own machinery for looking
after each other, on the working assumption that nobody else would. The
mutual-aid societies, the benevolent associations and the union halls they
founded never went away. They turned into the volunteer fire companies, the
credit unions, the burial societies and the social clubs, and those are still
the institutions that actually run the district's day-to-day life.

Engine Company 4 on Halloran Street has been a volunteer house since 1929 and
still keeps its own roll. The Nashoke Benevolent Association pays out on funeral
costs and has never advertised. The Grid Row Mutual, which is a credit union,
holds accounts for a substantial share of the district's households and is
frequently the only lender that will speak to them.

The civic league here is one of the best attended in the city. It meets monthly,
keeps minutes, and turns up at hearings in numbers. It holds no more legal
authority than any other civic league in Dawnbreak, which is none, and it is
consulted a great deal earlier than most.

### Underneath

The [[The Undercity|Undercity]] is denser beneath Grid Row than anywhere else,
because this is where the infrastructure is. Power runs, freight tunnels and
storm drainage all converge under these streets, some of it dating to the
original terminal works. The city describes the access points as utilities,
maintains them, and does not publish a map of them.

## H-Day and After

Grid Row was not under the release. The wind that morning was offshore and the
district is well northeast of the [[The Old Financial District|financial
district]], and the physical damage here was slight.

What Grid Row lost was people. The volunteer companies went in, along with every
other first-response service in the city, and first responders were affected out
of all proportion to their numbers for the obvious reason that they spent the
day inside the thing everybody else was leaving. Engine 4 lost members. So did
the two paid houses in the district. Twelve years on the roll boards in both
buildings still carry the 2028 names above the current ones, which is a house
tradition and not a memorial anybody asked permission for.

The district's population fell slightly in the years afterward and has not
recovered. Its institutions did not falter, which several people who study such
things have found notable, and which the district itself regards as the point of
having them.

## Who's There

Working households, many of them multi-generational, and a working-age
population employed in about equal parts by the utility, the terminal and the
trades. Rents are low and have stayed low. Turnover is the lowest in Dawnbreak.

**Dave Giannopoulos** grew up on these streets, worked out of a firehouse here,
and still lives in the district. That he also works as the Herculean is a matter
of public record and is treated locally with less ceremony than
[[Arkon Media]] would prefer.

**Amee Song** runs a free skating programme on Saturday mornings at the rink on
the district's inland edge. It is open to anyone under sixteen and has been
running for years.

Cross Idea Network operates an urgent care clinic here, open around the clock,
which is the closest thing the district has to a hospital and is used heavily.

## Incidents

**The freight gantry collapse, 2034.** A loading gantry in the rail yards failed
during a lift and came down across two tracks. The subsequent inspection found
the structure had been carrying loads above its rating for an extended period
and that the condition had been reported. The yards reopened in eleven days. The
report is public and the recommendations in it have been implemented in part.

## The Public Record

Load and output figures for the generating station are published quarterly. The
freight terminal publishes tonnage. The Harborworks alignment through the
district is a matter of public record, as is every hearing at which residents
objected to it, and the objections are recorded accurately in the minutes.

Fire company rolls, including the 2028 losses, are public. The credit union
files like any other credit union. The benevolent association files nothing
because it is not required to.

## Contested / unconfirmed

**Whether the wall was routed for the substations or against the houses.** The
engineering case for protecting the power supply is sound and nobody in the
district disputes it. What is disputed is whether an alignment protecting both
was ever costed, and the city has not produced one.

**How sealed the Undercity access points are.** The city maintains that the
entrances beneath Grid Row are locked, monitored and used only by utility crews.
People who live above them describe a good deal of traffic for a system that is
supposed to see none, and no one has ever produced a photograph.
