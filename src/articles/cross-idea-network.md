Cross Idea Network is an engineering firm. What it engineers, mostly, is medical
hardware, and if you have had something implanted in Dawnbreak in the last twenty
years there is a fair chance CIN designed it. The clinics with its name over the
door are the same company, and so is the workshop that machines the parts.

## Founding

Rennick J. Cross Sr. started the company in 1952. The Cross in Cross Idea
Network is his, and the name is a surname before it is a description of anything
the company does, which is not obvious from outside and is regularly misread.

He handed it to his son, Rennick J. Cross Jr., in 1994. It is still a family
company and there is no longer a Cross at the head of it. The company passed
from Cross Jr. to his nephew, David Dietrich, who owns it now and runs it as
chief executive. Three owners in eighty-eight years, and the same name on the
building for all of them.

Helena Marsh has been chief operating officer for eleven years and runs the
business day to day. The arrangement is not unusual for a company of this size
with an owner who did not build it.

## Structure

Four things sit under one name, and they are more connected than they look from
outside.

The engineering division is the company and everything else grew off it. It
designs medical devices, and implants in particular, which is specialised work
with a long regulatory tail and a small number of firms capable of doing it at
all.

The fabrication arm came out of that. A company designing implants ends up
needing parts made to tolerances nobody will supply on a reasonable schedule,
and the practical answer is to machine them yourself. CIN did, and then found it
had a fabrication business.

The clinics and the physical therapy practices are the end the public actually
touches. They are CIN-branded, they are ordinary working healthcare, and they
are the reason most people in Dawnbreak have heard the name at all.

## Operations

The CIN urgent care clinic in [[Grid Row]] is open around the clock and is used
heavily, being the closest thing that district has to a hospital. It is a
working clinic rather than a showroom, which is worth saying because a device
company running its own clinics invites the assumption.

The physical therapy practices work the same way. A firm that makes implants and
a firm that rehabilitates people who have had implants fitted are, in CIN's
case, the same firm, and the company has never been shy about pointing that out
in its own materials.

Dietrich has said publicly that he intends to sponsor a team of up-and-coming
heroes. That puts CIN in the same business as [[Arkon Media]] on a very much
smaller scale, and under the same incentives: sponsoring hero work earns a
company tax relief, grants and access to government contracts, and the structure
does not change for being applied to a smaller firm.

## In the City

The Cross Idea Network tower stands in [[New Haven]], within sight of the Arkon
Media campus and close enough to watch it fill up for an announcement. The lobby
is sleek and modern and very clean, close to futuristic, with soft music coming
through the intercom. It is a building that has had money spent on the
impression it makes.

CIN and Arkon are not rivals in any sense either company performs. One is a
media conglomerate that owns the city's news and its most famous residents, the
other is an engineering firm with clinics, and where their interests overlap neither
has ever made anything of it. There has never been a public quarrel between
them.

The distinction worth holding on to is that CIN is not an alternative to the
arrangement Arkon represents. It is a participant in it.

## The Public Record

CIN is privately held, and a privately held company files a good deal less than
a public one. What is on the record is what regulation puts there. The clinics
are licensed and inspected like any other clinics. The device work sits under
the approval regime that all device work sits under, and that paperwork is
public.

The fabrication side is where the record thins out. What CIN makes on contract,
and who for, is not published, and the company has not been asked about it in
any forum that produced an answer.
